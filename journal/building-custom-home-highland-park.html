Building a custom home in Highland Park, Texas: what to know

By Seb Pessy, Founder of Sebby Homes · Updated July 2026

Building a custom home in Highland Park means working with premium land, comparatively tight lots, mature trees, and one of the most rigorous town review processes in Dallas–Fort Worth. The owners who build well here start with a builder who plans for those realities from the very first drawing.

Highland Park is among the most sought-after addresses in Texas, and it rewards precision. As a design-led builder working across the Park Cities, Sebby Homes approaches a Highland Park project differently than an open suburban lot. Here is what we tell owners before they begin.

1. The lot drives the design

Highland Park lots are valuable and often tighter than newer DFW neighborhoods. Setbacks, lot coverage, height limits, and mature trees shape what is possible, so the smartest designs begin with a careful read of the site, not a floor plan borrowed from elsewhere.

2. Town review is rigorous: plan for it

The Town of Highland Park maintains high standards for design and construction, and its review and permitting process is more involved than many surrounding cities. A builder fluent in the process sets expectations early and avoids the resubmittals that quietly add months.

3. Land is a large share of the budget

In Highland Park, the lot can rival or exceed the cost of the house. That makes the construction budget a decision about value per square foot, not just size: a case for the design-led, budget-integrated approach we describe in our guide to custom home cost in DFW.

4. Neighbors and streetscape matter

Building on an established street means managing a clean, respectful site and a contemporary home that sits confidently among its neighbors. Disciplined site leadership is part of the craft here, not an afterthought.

In Highland Park, the constraints are the brief. Great homes here are designed with the lot, the town, and the street in mind from day one.
