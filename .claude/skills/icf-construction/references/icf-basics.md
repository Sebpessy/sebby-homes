# ICF Basics — Anatomy, Variation Points, Estimating Structure

## Anatomy
- **Form types**: flat wall (uniform concrete thickness — most common in
  residential), waffle-grid, screen-grid. IRC R608 treats them separately.
- **Block**: two EPS panels connected by plastic (or steel) webs. Webs
  provide furring strips for drywall/siding attachment — spacing is
  brand-specific.
- **Core sizes**: commonly 4"/6"/8" concrete cores; exact core and foam
  thickness vary by brand. "6-inch ICF" from two brands can use different
  concrete volumes — always use the brand's stated yield.
- **Specialty blocks**: 90° and 45° corners, brick ledge, taper-top,
  height adjusters, T-blocks. Corner counts drive real cost — count them.
- **Bucks**: window/door frames (wood, PVC, or brand buck product) that
  form the opening and provide attachment.

## What to verify in manufacturer literature (per project)
1. Block face dimensions and per-block wall area (for counts)
2. Concrete yield per block or per SF for the core selected
3. Reinforcement placement geometry (web notches set bar positions)
4. Lift height and pour rate limits; slump/aggregate spec
5. Their engineered wall tables — many brands publish engineering manuals
   that function like span tables; current edition only, and confirm the
   AHJ accepts them
6. Attachment hardware catalog (ledger connectors, embed plates)
7. ESR/code report number for the AHJ packet

## Estimating structure (lines, not prices)
- ICF blocks + corners + specialty + freight
- Bucks and buck bracing lumber
- Rebar (by size, from the governing table) + chairs/ties + dowels
- Concrete (yield × area + waste; pump minimums)
- Pump (line vs boom, hours/minimums)
- Bracing/alignment rental (per section per week) + scaffolding plank
- Labor: stack/brace/pour crew days; first-timer premium is real —
  price experienced ICF labor or budget the brand's tech rep + slower crew
- Waterproofing/dampproofing below grade + termite inspection strip
- Fasteners/foam gun/adhesive consumables
- Pre-pour engineering/inspection if engineered path

## Texas notes
- Hail/tornado resilience is the sales angle; pair the estimate with an
  insurance-premium conversation for the client.
- Below-grade foam and termites: check local amendments before promising
  a full-height below-grade ICF basement detail.
- Hot-weather concreting practices apply to summer pours (retarder,
  earlier start, ice as needed — per the ready-mix supplier).
